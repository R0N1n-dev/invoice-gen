Four ways to send a bill.

Same product, same screens — dashboard, invoice list, editor, PDF preview. Pick the one that matches how you want to work; the chosen direction becomes the full design system (tokens, PDF layout, Android status bar and splash colours).
Direction A

Ledger

Warm paper, ink and a single forest green. A serif voice for money, hairline rules instead of boxes, tabular figures everywhere. Reads like a well-kept account book — calm, expensive, accountant-approved.

  • Editorial & quiet
  • Serif numerals
  • Low chrome
  • Best for: studios, consultants

Direction B

Slate

Dark, dense and operational. A gradient hero card carries the outstanding balance, mono figures keep columns honest, status lives on a thin colour rail. Built for scanning a lot of invoices quickly, in bed, at 2am.

  • Dark-first
  • Data dense
  • Mono figures
  • Best for: agencies, high volume
Open prototype →
Direction C

Bloom

Soft lilac and peach, generous radii, floating pill navigation, a collection ring instead of a chart. Friendly without being childish — the direction that makes invoicing feel like less of a chore.

  • Soft & friendly
  • Floating nav
  • Rounded cards
  • Best for: freelancers, creatives
Open prototype →
Direction D

Swiss

Strict grid, black hairlines, monospaced labels and one vermilion accent. Everything is a table, nothing is a card. Maximum legibility, minimum decoration — the invoice as a document, not an app.

  • Brutalist grid
  • Mono labels
  • Zero radius
  • Best for: print-minded, engineering
